# D12 — Who may act, and for whom

Status: DECIDED

## Why this needs Cory

Whether everyone in the first slice acts only for themselves.

- In the D01 scenario, does every person sign only for themselves?
- If a client, brand, agency or company is involved, who signs for it, and what establishes that they may?
- May anyone commit another person?

Authority is never inferred from controlling an account or an inbox.

**[F] lean — a recommendation, not a default.** For the first slice, adults acting for themselves only, with organizations and representatives added when designed. This is a proposed constraint that needs Cory's approval. If the D01 scenario includes someone who signs for another party, it does not apply.

## Already settled

Nothing for the adult side.

- Guardian-managed profiles for minors are ratified **[R]** (DOCTRINE:86).
- Youth launch is deferred pending professional legal review **[R]** (VISION:463; DOCTRINE:89). That settles the deferral, not the youth design.
- Agents and agencies appear in July tier thinking **[CA]** (CORY-QUESTIONS:163).

## Cory's answer

Direct ruling — 2026-10-05.

No proxies. Nobody signs on another person's behalf. The person acting is the person reading and affirming.

Clients and other non-present parties sign their own applicable documentation. Being the creator, organizer, client, payer or account holder never authorizes someone to affirm for another person.

For the current adult collaboration path, minors are out of scope.

Cory also described witness signatures as potentially useful and optional. Witnessing is a separate evidence feature; it does not create proxy authority and is not required by this decision.

The long-term status of the previously ratified deferred youth partition is not changed here. Cory's current statement governs the present build; any permanent removal of the future youth path should be reconciled explicitly against I9 before changing that canon.
