### Direct rulings — 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-14/15

Source: [`2026-10-09-15-cory-reconciliation.md`](2026-10-09-15-cory-reconciliation.md) (B2, Q14, C3).

**The question "which documents are essential" was rejected as posed — there is no fixed essential set.** "It's absolutely case by case depending on what's checked during the creation of the brief."

- **Documents attach automatically from what is checked.** No default bundle. A model release may not even be attached. Redundant paperwork defeats the point.
- **The toolbox stays:** every document type is available in a list to add manually for anything the triggers miss.
- **Releases are directional and independent.** Her using his image and him using her likeness are two separate grants; granting one never grants the other.
- **No minor release** in the current adult product (see D12).
- **NDAs are real and may be time-bounded** — shows in preparation, unreleased work, confidential until advertised.
- **The document trigger table is a draft Dustin writes and Cory checks against real briefs**, not something approved in the abstract.
- The 2026-10-05 ruling still stands for the D01 scenario: its contemplated album-cover use triggers a photo release.

**The call sheet is a document the product produces.** (Q14) Shared logistics is a first-class layer: schedule, call times and location belong there, and everyone involved sees it, including each other's time slots (visibility in D10). Cory wants the call sheet built as a real document, not merely a screen or an incidental export. Its schema and template are not designed here.

**Counsel, still open:** which templates need professional legal review (C3) — not a Cory instinct question. No legal templates are drafted here.

Cross-cutting: the new standard, not the existing one — industry omissions are not a design authority (doctrine 3).
