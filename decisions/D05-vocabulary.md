# D05 — Vocabulary

Status: OPEN — words for a presented version, a response and the archived states remain

## Why this needs Cory

The words people see. Cory chooses them for:

- the collaboration container;
- the terms as presented;
- a participant's response;
- the finalized agreement;
- the artifact each party holds;
- the statuses for archived and completed.

A name must not promise more than the state delivers. "Agreed", "final", "signed" and "verified" each claim something.

## Already settled

- The product name and "guardian manager" **[R]**.
- The ratified documents use "brief" for the terms document throughout.
- The product claims only what it does **[R]** (DOCTRINE:93; invariant I7 in `planning/B-product-invariants.md`).

## Cory's answer

Partial direct ruling — 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-14/15. Source: [`2026-10-09-15-cory-reconciliation.md`](2026-10-09-15-cory-reconciliation.md) (Q18, B1).

- **Brief before signing. Contract after.** The thing changes, so the word changes — before signature it genuinely is not a contract. This satisfies "a name must not promise more than the state delivers."
- **What each person walks away holding: the contract.**
- **The container has no blanket public name.** Things are labelled by what they are — boudoir shoot, fine art shoot, video shoot — drawn from the brief. Nobody ever sees "Project 47." Internal code names do not wait on this.
- **Document names keep their real names** — model release, photography contract. That is where the search volume lives, so the public pages own those names regardless. "Brief" is uncontested in this space, so it is cheaper to own than to compete for.
- **Model release and photo release are distinct documents.** "Photo release" is genuinely ambiguous in the field; this product has both.
- Research note Cory accepted: the industry splits brief (creative plan), contract (business terms) and release (likeness). Modeling Standard merges them, so the merged object has no existing name.

**Still open:** the words for **a presented version**, **a response**, and **the archived states**. Cory wants these **drafted in context** — on a real screen, in a real email — for him to react to, not generated cold. No label is supplied here.

## What this unlocks

- Internal code names are not product vocabulary and do not wait for this.
